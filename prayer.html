Learn how to pray before and after Bible reading, and how to keep praying during the day.

Prayer section

Everything you need to start praying with Scripture and honesty.

This page boots directly into the Prayer section from the reference guide so you can stay focused on the practice instead of the rest of the library.

1

How often?

Pray daily, then add short prayers during ordinary moments so it becomes a habit instead of a project.

2

Before reading?

Yes. Pray briefly before Bible reading for attention and humility, then pray again after reading.

3

Does the Bible have prayers?

Yes. Psalms, Jesus' prayers, the apostles' prayers, and many stories show how Scripture teaches prayer.

4

What about written prayers?

Use them as training wheels. Read slowly, pray honestly, then add your own words instead of copying them blindly. Book of Common Worship prayers can help when you need a starting point.

Simple rhythm

Pray briefly before reading, then pray back one thing after reading.

That keeps prayer tied to Scripture and makes the habit small enough to repeat tomorrow.

Try this

A one-minute prayer you can use before Bible reading.

If you do not know what to say, start here and then change the words slowly to fit your own situation.

God, thank you for this day. Help me hear your word clearly. Show me what I need to confess, what I need to trust, and what I need to do. Teach me to pray honestly and to follow Jesus more closely. Amen.

After reading

A short prayer you can use after Bible reading.

Read one passage, notice one thing that stood out, and turn that into a prayer instead of moving on immediately.

Lord Jesus, thank you for speaking to me through your word. Help me trust what is true, obey what is clear, and remember what matters. Keep your word in my mind today and shape my thoughts, words, and actions. Amen.

Using a prayer book

How to use Common Worship or another written prayer book.

Written prayers are most useful when they help you begin honestly, not when they replace your own prayer.

1

Read slowly

Pray one line at a time instead of rushing to the end. Let the words shape your attention.

2

Pause and respond

After a line or a paragraph, stop and add your own sentence so the prayer stays personal.

3

Use it as a guide

Keep the book nearby for when your own words feel thin, then return to Scripture and your own voice.

A simple example

1. Read a written prayer slowly.

2. Pause at one sentence that stands out.

3. Add one sentence of your own.

4. End with Amen and move on with your day.

With liturgy

How to pray when you are using a set worship service.

A liturgy can carry you when you do not have words. Join in slowly, repeat what you can, and mean what you say.

1

Follow the order

Let the service guide you instead of trying to improvise every part.

2

Say the words slowly

Speak the prayers in a measured way so the meaning can land.

3

Join with intention

Use the written words as your own prayer for that moment, not as a script to rush through.

A simple example

Listen, repeat the prayer slowly, add your Amen, and let the service do some of the work for you.

Daily rhythm

A simple prayer pattern for the whole day.

You do not need a perfect schedule. Start small, repeat the pattern, and let prayer become part of ordinary life.

Morning

Start the day

Thank God for waking you, ask for help with the day ahead, and read a short passage if you can.

Midday

Pause and remember

Use a meal, a walk, or a quiet break to pray one short sentence: thank you, help me, or teach me.

Evening

Close the day

Review the day honestly, confess what needs confession, give thanks for mercy, and rest in God’s care.

Pray a Psalm

How to turn a Psalm into prayer.

If a Psalm feels strange, do not wait to understand every line. Read it slowly and pray the parts that match your life.

1

Read aloud

Read a short Psalm out loud, even if you only read a few verses.

2

Notice the words

Pick one phrase that sounds like praise, confession, lament, trust, or thanks.

3

Pray it back

Turn that phrase into your own sentence and speak it back to God in your own words.

Pray for others

How to pray when someone else needs help.

Intercession means asking God for someone else. It can be as short as one sentence.

1

Name the person

Say who you are praying for and what they need, if you know it.

2

Ask God plainly

Use simple words: help them, heal them, give them peace, or give them wisdom.

3

Keep it short

You do not need a long speech. One honest sentence is enough to begin.

A simple example

God, please help my friend today. Give them peace, wisdom, and the support they need. Amen.

Five minutes

A fuller starter prayer flow for a new day.

If one minute feels too short and a full liturgy feels too heavy, use this middle-sized pattern.

1

Begin with thanks

Thank God for life, mercy, and the chance to start again today.

2

Tell the truth

Confess where you were distracted, proud, afraid, or unkind, and ask for help.

3

Ask for others

Pray for one or two other people by name before you move on with your day.

Example outline

Thank God, confess honestly, ask for help, pray for others, read a Psalm, and end with Amen.

When anxious

How to pray when you feel worried or overwhelmed.

Anxiety can make prayer feel hard. Keep it small and direct: name the fear, ask for help, and return to trust.

1

Name the fear

Tell God exactly what feels heavy instead of trying to sound calm.

2

Ask for peace

Ask God for peace, clarity, strength, or help with the next step.

3

Use Scripture

Read a Psalm, the Lord's Prayer, or a short verse, then pray it back slowly.

A simple example

God, I am worried and tired. Please give me peace, show me the next right step, and help me trust you today. Amen.

When dry

How to pray when you do not feel much at all.

Prayer does not depend on strong feelings. If you feel stuck, use a small honest sentence and a Psalm.

1

Say that plainly

Tell God you feel dry, distracted, or numb. Honesty is enough to begin.

2

Borrow words

Read a Psalm or a written prayer and let it carry you when your own words feel thin.

3

Stay brief

Do not force intensity. A short prayer repeated faithfully is better than a forced speech.

A simple example

God, I do not have many words today. Help me stay near you, and let your Psalm shape my prayer. Amen.

With others

How to pray with another person or a small group.

Group prayer does not need to sound impressive. Keep it simple, brief, and centered on God rather than performance.

1

Share a request

Let each person say one thing they need prayer for.

2

Take turns briefly

Pray one short prayer at a time so nobody has to carry the whole moment.

3

End with thanks

Thank God together for hearing you, then move on with the day or meeting.

A simple example

God, thank you for this person and their needs. Please give them peace, wisdom, and help. Amen.

Confession

How to pray when you know you have done wrong.

Confession is not about hiding less and pretending more. It is about telling God the truth and asking for mercy.

1

Name the sin

Be specific about what was wrong instead of speaking vaguely.

2

Ask for mercy

Ask God to forgive you and help you turn around.

3

Receive grace

Thank God for mercy, then take the next right step in obedience.

A simple example

God, I was proud and unkind. Please forgive me, change me, and help me do better today. Amen.

Forgiveness

How to pray when you need help forgiving someone.

Forgiveness can take time. Prayer can start by asking God to soften your heart and help you release revenge.

1

Name the hurt

Tell God what happened and why it still hurts.

2

Ask for help

Ask God to help you release bitterness and to act with wisdom and honesty.

3

Take a next step

Pray for the person, set a boundary if needed, and keep asking God for a free heart.

A simple example

God, this hurt me. Help me forgive, keep me from revenge, and lead me toward peace and wisdom. Amen.

Thanksgiving

How to pray when you want to thank God.

Thanksgiving keeps prayer from becoming only a list of requests. It trains you to notice mercy and gift.

1

Name the gift

Thank God for one concrete thing: a meal, a person, a breath, or a piece of help.

2

Say why it matters

Connect the gift to God's care instead of treating it as random luck.

3

Keep noticing

Let gratitude become a habit during meals, walks, and the end of the day.

A simple example

God, thank you for this day, for this food, and for the people who care for me. Amen.

Praise

How to pray when you want to worship God for who he is.

Praise is different from thanksgiving. Thanksgiving names gifts; praise names God's character, power, and goodness.

1

Name who God is

Use words like holy, faithful, good, merciful, strong, or wise.

2

Adore before asking

Let worship come first so your prayer is rooted in God, not just your need.

3

Use Scripture language

Let Psalms and the Lord's Prayer give you words for wonder and reverence.

A simple example

God, you are holy, faithful, and full of mercy. I worship you and trust your goodness today. Amen.

The Lord's Prayer

How to pray the Lord's Prayer slowly and honestly.

Do not rush it. Let each line shape your attention: God first, then your needs, then forgiveness and protection.

1

Begin with God

Start with reverence for God's name and a desire for his kingdom.

2

Ask for daily needs

Pray for bread, forgiveness, and help with temptation in plain words.

3

Repeat slowly

Use the prayer as a pattern, then pause and add your own sentence where needed.

A simple example

Read one line, pause, speak it again in your own words, and end with Amen.

Breath prayer

How to pray a very short prayer in one breath.

Breath prayers are useful when you are walking, waiting, or need a quick reminder that God is near.

1

Choose a short phrase

Use one phrase from Scripture or a simple request like, Lord Jesus, have mercy.

2

Repeat with breath

Say the prayer as you breathe in and out, slowly and without forcing it.

3

Use it often

Return to it during walks, errands, or anxious moments as a small anchor.

A simple example

Lord Jesus, have mercy. Lord Jesus, have mercy.

Guidance

How to pray when you need wisdom for a decision.

You do not always get instant clarity, but you can ask God for wisdom, wait, and keep choosing what is faithful.

1

Name the decision

Tell God exactly what choice you are facing and why it matters.

2

Ask for wisdom

Ask for clarity, patience, and the courage to do the right thing.

3

Wait and act

Read Scripture, seek counsel if needed, and take the next faithful step.

A simple example

God, I need wisdom about this decision. Show me what is true, help me trust you, and lead me in the next right step. Amen.

Silence

How to pray by sitting quietly before God.

Prayer is not always many words. Sometimes it is attention, stillness, and a willingness to listen.

1

Set a short timer

Take one or two minutes to sit still and stop trying to perform.

2

Turn attention to God

Breathe slowly, notice distractions, and gently return your attention to God.

3

End with one sentence

After the silence, speak one honest sentence of thanks, trust, or request.

A simple example

Lord, I am here. Help me listen, trust you, and rest in your care. Amen.

Prayer list

How to keep a simple prayer list or journal.

Writing requests down helps you remember who and what you are praying for, and it lets you notice answers later.

1

Write names and needs

Keep a small list of people, decisions, hurts, and things you are waiting on.

2

Pray from the list

Move through it slowly and keep the prayers short and specific.

3

Look back later

Review the list to notice what changed, what remained, and where God provided help.

A simple example

Friend: peace. Family: patience. Decision: wisdom. Body: healing. Thank God for what he has already done.

Meals and moments

How to pray during meals, walks, and interruptions.

Prayer does not need a special room. Ordinary moments can become prayer when you pause and turn toward God.

1

Before meals

Thank God for the food and the people around you, then ask for blessing or peace.

2

During walks

Use a few steps or a few minutes to pray one sentence at a time.

3

In interruptions

When something surprises you, pause and say, help me, thank you, or have mercy.

A simple example

God, thank you for this meal. Bless the people here, and help me carry your peace into the next part of the day. Amen.
