Prayer guide

Learn how to pray before and after Bible reading, and how to keep praying during the day.

Start with honest words to God, use the Psalms and the Lord's Prayer, and build a simple rhythm that supports reading Scripture instead of competing with it.

Prayer section

Everything you need to start praying with Scripture and honesty.

This page is a direct prayer guide, not a browser. Use the four questions below as your starting point, then move through the practical sections that follow.

1

How often?

Pray daily, then add short prayers during ordinary moments so it becomes a habit instead of a project.

2

Before reading?

Yes. Pray briefly before Bible reading for attention and humility, then pray again after reading.

3

Does the Bible have prayers?

Yes. Psalms, Jesus' prayers, the apostles' prayers, and many stories show how Scripture teaches prayer.

4

What about written prayers?

Use them as training wheels. Read slowly, pray honestly, then add your own words instead of copying them blindly. Book of Common Worship prayers can help when you need a starting point.

Simple rhythm

Pray briefly before reading, then pray back one thing after reading.

That keeps prayer tied to Scripture and makes the habit small enough to repeat tomorrow.

Try this

A one-minute prayer you can use before Bible reading.

If you do not know what to say, start here and then change the words slowly to fit your own situation.

God, thank you for this day. Help me hear your word clearly. Show me what I need to confess, what I need to trust, and what I need to do. Teach me to pray honestly and to follow Jesus more closely. Amen.

After reading

A short prayer you can use after Bible reading.

Read one passage, notice one thing that stood out, and turn that into a prayer instead of moving on immediately.

Lord Jesus, thank you for speaking to me through your word. Help me trust what is true, obey what is clear, and remember what matters. Keep your word in my mind today and shape my thoughts, words, and actions. Amen.

With liturgy

How to pray when you are using a set worship service.

A liturgy can carry you when you do not have words. Join in slowly, repeat what you can, and mean what you say.

1

Follow the order

Let the service guide you instead of trying to improvise every part.

2

Say the words slowly

Speak the prayers in a measured way so the meaning can land.

3

Join with intention

Use the written words as your own prayer for that moment, not as a script to rush through.

A simple example

Listen, repeat the prayer slowly, add your Amen, and let the service do some of the work for you.

Before worship

How to pray before you go to church or join a worship service.

A short prayer before worship can prepare your heart to listen, sing, confess, and receive what God gives in the gathering.

1

Ask for attention

Pray that God would help you focus and not drift through the service.

2

Ask for humility

Invite God to soften your heart so you can hear, repent, and receive grace.

3

Join with the body

Pray for the people gathering with you and for the service to help everyone worship well.

A simple example

God, prepare my heart for worship. Help me listen well, sing honestly, and receive your grace with humility. Amen.

After worship

How to pray after church or a worship service.

After worship, take a minute to thank God for what you heard, confess what you resisted, and ask him to help you live it out.

1

Thank God for what landed

Name one song, prayer, or word that helped you notice God.

2

Confess resistance

Ask forgiveness for anything you ignored, doubted, or held back from receiving.

3

Pray it outward

Ask God to turn the service into obedience, love, and witness in the week ahead.

A simple example

Lord, thank you for speaking to me today. Help me remember what mattered and live it out this week. Amen.

Daily rhythm

A simple prayer pattern for the whole day.

You do not need a perfect schedule. Start small, repeat the pattern, and let prayer become part of ordinary life.

Morning

Start the day

Thank God for waking you, ask for help with the day ahead, and read a short passage if you can.

Midday

Pause and remember

Use a meal, a walk, or a quiet break to pray one short sentence: thank you, help me, or teach me.

Evening

Close the day

Review the day honestly, confess what needs confession, give thanks for mercy, and rest in God’s care.

Bedtime

How to pray before sleep.

Bedtime prayer can be short and quiet. It helps you close the day with thanks, confession, and trust instead of carrying everything into sleep.

1

Thank God for the day

Notice one gift from the day before you go to sleep.

2

Confess and release

Name anything that needs forgiveness, then ask God to carry what you cannot fix tonight.

3

Ask for rest

Pray for peace, protection, and real rest for the night ahead.

A simple example

Lord, thank you for today. Forgive what was wrong, hold what is unfinished, and give me peaceful rest tonight. Amen.

Morning

How to pray when you wake up.

Morning prayer does not need much time. It can be the first words of the day: thanks, surrender, and a request for help.

1

Thank God first

Begin with gratitude for waking, for mercy, and for a new day.

2

Surrender the day

Ask God to lead your words, work, and relationships before the day gets busy.

3

Read a short passage

Open Scripture if you can, then pray one thing back from what you read.

A simple example

God, thank you for this new day. Lead me, guard me, and help me follow Jesus in what comes next. Amen.

Fasting

How to pray when you are fasting or giving something up.

Fasting is not about proving something. It is about making room for prayer, humility, and dependence on God.

1

Keep it simple

Choose a fast you can actually keep, and keep the reason clear.

2

Pray when you notice hunger

Let meals or cravings remind you to ask God for help and to read a Psalm.

3

End with gratitude

When the fast ends, thank God for the time of prayer and the provision he gives.

A simple example

Lord, make room in me for prayer and humility. Help me depend on you more than on comfort. Amen.

Healing

How to pray when you or someone else is sick.

Praying for healing is simple: ask God to heal, give strength, bring wise care, and hold the person in peace.

1

Name the need

Say who is sick and what kind of help they need.

2

Ask for healing

Pray for recovery, relief, strength, and comfort.

3

Support with care

Pair prayer with practical help, medical care, and presence where you can.

A simple example

God, please heal this person, relieve their pain, and give strength and wisdom to everyone caring for them. Amen.

Pray a Psalm

How to turn a Psalm into prayer.

If a Psalm feels strange, do not wait to understand every line. Read it slowly and pray the parts that match your life.

1

Read aloud

Read a short Psalm out loud, even if you only read a few verses.

2

Notice the words

Pick one phrase that sounds like praise, confession, lament, trust, or thanks.

3

Pray it back

Turn that phrase into your own sentence and speak it back to God in your own words.

Pray for others

How to pray when someone else needs help.

Intercession means asking God for someone else. It can be as short as one sentence.

1

Name the person

Say who you are praying for and what they need, if you know it.

2

Ask God plainly

Use simple words: help them, heal them, give them peace, or give them wisdom.

3

Keep it short

You do not need a long speech. One honest sentence is enough to begin.

A simple example

God, please help my friend today. Give them peace, wisdom, and the support they need. Amen.

Five minutes

A fuller starter prayer flow for a new day.

If one minute feels too short and a full liturgy feels too heavy, use this middle-sized pattern.

1

Begin with thanks

Thank God for life, mercy, and the chance to start again today.

2

Tell the truth

Confess where you were distracted, proud, afraid, or unkind, and ask for help.

3

Ask for others

Pray for one or two other people by name before you move on with your day.

Example outline

Thank God, confess honestly, ask for help, pray for others, read a Psalm, and end with Amen.

When anxious

How to pray when you feel worried or overwhelmed.

Anxiety can make prayer feel hard. Keep it small and direct: name the fear, ask for help, and return to trust.

1

Name the fear

Tell God exactly what feels heavy instead of trying to sound calm.

2

Ask for peace

Ask God for peace, clarity, strength, or help with the next step.

3

Use Scripture

Read a Psalm, the Lord's Prayer, or a short verse, then pray it back slowly.

A simple example

God, I am worried and tired. Please give me peace, show me the next right step, and help me trust you today. Amen.

When dry

How to pray when you do not feel much at all.

Prayer does not depend on strong feelings. If you feel stuck, use a small honest sentence and a Psalm.

1

Say that plainly

Tell God you feel dry, distracted, or numb. Honesty is enough to begin.

2

Borrow words

Read a Psalm or a written prayer and let it carry you when your own words feel thin.

3

Stay brief

Do not force intensity. A short prayer repeated faithfully is better than a forced speech.

A simple example

God, I do not have many words today. Help me stay near you, and let your Psalm shape my prayer. Amen.

When faith feels weak

How to pray when you want to believe, but doubt is loud.

Doubt is not solved by pretending. Bring it to God honestly, ask for help, and keep showing up with the faith you do have.

1

Say the doubt out loud

Tell God what is hard to believe or what feels uncertain.

2

Ask for help to believe

Pray for trust, clarity, and patience while you wait for understanding.

3

Keep turning back

Use Scripture, prayer book words, or the Lord's Prayer to stay near God while you wait.

A simple example

Lord, I believe, but help my unbelief. Keep me near you and help me trust what is true. Amen.

Scripture memory

How to pray with a verse you are learning by heart.

Memorized Scripture can become prayer when you repeat it slowly, reflect on it, and turn it back toward God.

1

Pick one short verse

Choose a verse or Psalm line that already speaks to your need or question.

2

Repeat it slowly

Say the verse aloud a few times and notice which word or phrase stays with you.

3

Pray it back

Turn the verse into a prayer by asking God to make it true in you or through you.

A simple example

Lord, let your word dwell in me. Teach me to trust what you say and to live it out today. Amen.

With others

How to pray with another person or a small group.

Group prayer does not need to sound impressive. Keep it simple, brief, and centered on God rather than performance.

1

Share a request

Let each person say one thing they need prayer for.

2

Take turns briefly

Pray one short prayer at a time so nobody has to carry the whole moment.

3

End with thanks

Thank God together for hearing you, then move on with the day or meeting.

A simple example

God, thank you for this person and their needs. Please give them peace, wisdom, and help. Amen.

Confession

How to pray when you know you have done wrong.

Confession is not about hiding less and pretending more. It is about telling God the truth and asking for mercy.

1

Name the sin

Be specific about what was wrong instead of speaking vaguely.

2

Ask for mercy

Ask God to forgive you and help you turn around.

3

Receive grace

Thank God for mercy, then take the next right step in obedience.

A simple example

God, I was proud and unkind. Please forgive me, change me, and help me do better today. Amen.

Recurring sin

How to pray when you keep falling into the same sin.

Repeated failure can make prayer feel discouraging. Keep coming back with honesty, ask for help, and take one concrete step away from the pattern.

1

Tell the truth again

Name the pattern without excusing it or drowning in shame.

2

Ask for help to change

Pray for repentance, accountability, and the strength to interrupt the habit.

3

Take one practical step

Remove a trigger, ask for support, or change your routine where the sin tends to start.

A simple example

God, I keep falling back into this sin. Forgive me, give me strength to change, and show me the next faithful step. Amen.

Amends

How to pray when you need to make things right with someone.

If your sin hurt another person, prayer should move toward repair: honest apology, humble listening, and the next concrete step toward peace.

1

Pray before you speak

Ask God for humility, courage, and the right words before you reach out.

2

Make the apology plain

Say what you did, own the harm, and do not hide behind excuses.

3

Pray for repair

Ask God to help you listen, make restitution if needed, and do what peace requires.

A simple example

God, help me own what I did and make things right. Give me humility, honesty, and the courage to repair the damage I caused. Amen.

Forgiveness

How to pray when you need help forgiving someone.

Forgiveness can take time. Prayer can start by asking God to soften your heart and help you release revenge.

1

Name the hurt

Tell God what happened and why it still hurts.

2

Ask for help

Ask God to help you release bitterness and to act with wisdom and honesty.

3

Take a next step

Pray for the person, set a boundary if needed, and keep asking God for a free heart.

A simple example

God, this hurt me. Help me forgive, keep me from revenge, and lead me toward peace and wisdom. Amen.

For enemies

How to pray for the people who oppose or hurt you.

Jesus tells his followers to pray for enemies, which means asking God for their good without pretending the hurt did not happen.

1

Name the person honestly

Bring the actual person or situation to God without hiding your anger or fear.

2

Ask for their good

Pray for repentance, peace, wisdom, or whatever good would truly help them and others.

3

Guard your heart

Ask God to keep you from revenge, hardening, or bitterness while he works.

A simple example

Lord, help me not to harden my heart. Please do what is right in this person’s life, and keep me faithful, honest, and free from revenge. Amen.

Thanksgiving

How to pray when you want to thank God.

Thanksgiving keeps prayer from becoming only a list of requests. It trains you to notice mercy and gift.

1

Name the gift

Thank God for one concrete thing: a meal, a person, a breath, or a piece of help.

2

Say why it matters

Connect the gift to God's care instead of treating it as random luck.

3

Keep noticing

Let gratitude become a habit during meals, walks, and the end of the day.

A simple example

God, thank you for this day, for this food, and for the people who care for me. Amen.

Praise

How to pray when you want to worship God for who he is.

Praise is different from thanksgiving. Thanksgiving names gifts; praise names God's character, power, and goodness.

1

Name who God is

Use words like holy, faithful, good, merciful, strong, or wise.

2

Adore before asking

Let worship come first so your prayer is rooted in God, not just your need.

3

Use Scripture language

Let Psalms and the Lord's Prayer give you words for wonder and reverence.

A simple example

God, you are holy, faithful, and full of mercy. I worship you and trust your goodness today. Amen.

The Lord's Prayer

How to pray the Lord's Prayer slowly and honestly.

Do not rush it. Let each line shape your attention: God first, then your needs, then forgiveness and protection.

1

Begin with God

Start with reverence for God's name and a desire for his kingdom.

2

Ask for daily needs

Pray for bread, forgiveness, and help with temptation in plain words.

3

Repeat slowly

Use the prayer as a pattern, then pause and add your own sentence where needed.

A simple example

Read one line, pause, speak it again in your own words, and end with Amen.

Scripted prayer

How to grow into fuller written prayers.

Some prayers are meant to be recited with more form. Scripted prayer can slow you down, teach you language, and help you pray when you want more than a one-sentence request.

1

Follow the shape

Notice the order of praise, confession, thanksgiving, and request instead of rushing through the words.

2

Pray line by line

Read each line slowly and let it become your prayer before moving to the next one.

3

Keep the words

Memorize a few prayers that help you when your own words are thin or scattered.

A simple example

Start with a short written prayer, read it aloud, pause after each sentence, and repeat it again the next day.

Psalm recitation

How to pray by reciting Psalms more fully.

Psalms are not only for quick prompts. You can learn to pray whole portions aloud so the Bible itself shapes your voice and memory.

1

Read a larger chunk

Take a Psalm in its own section, not just one verse, and let the flow of the prayer carry you.

2

Recite aloud

Speak the Psalm slowly so the language, repetition, and movement stay in your mind.

3

Answer with Amen

After the Psalm, pause and answer with one sentence of praise, lament, or trust.

A simple example

Read Psalm 23 aloud, pause after each line, and end by saying, Lord, be my shepherd today. Amen.

Liturgical prayer

How to pray with a set form and a gathered church.

Liturgy gives you a prayer rhythm bigger than your own mood. It teaches you to pray with the church across time, not only in private moments.

1

Follow the pattern

Let the order of the service lead you through praise, confession, Scripture, prayer, and response.

2

Join the words

Speak the shared prayer as your own instead of treating it like background text.

3

Carry it home

Use the same shape later in the week so church prayer becomes part of daily life.

A simple example

Use the service as written, listen for the repeated patterns, and let the gathered prayer teach you how to pray on your own.

Temptation

How to pray when you feel pulled toward something you should avoid.

Temptation usually needs a quick prayer, a clear boundary, and a return to what is true before the moment gets bigger.

1

Name it quickly

Say what is tempting you instead of pretending it is harmless.

2

Ask for escape

Pray for strength, a way out, and the willingness to take it.

3

Move your body

Step away, put the device down, close the tab, or change the situation while you pray.

A simple example

God, I am being tempted right now. Give me strength, show me the way out, and help me choose what is good. Amen.

Breath prayer

How to pray a very short prayer in one breath.

Breath prayers are useful when you are walking, waiting, or need a quick reminder that God is near.

1

Choose a short phrase

Use one phrase from Scripture or a simple request like, Lord Jesus, have mercy.

2

Repeat with breath

Say the prayer as you breathe in and out, slowly and without forcing it.

3

Use it often

Return to it during walks, errands, or anxious moments as a small anchor.

A simple example

Lord Jesus, have mercy. Lord Jesus, have mercy.

Guidance

How to pray when you need wisdom for a decision.

You do not always get instant clarity, but you can ask God for wisdom, wait, and keep choosing what is faithful.

1

Name the decision

Tell God exactly what choice you are facing and why it matters.

2

Ask for wisdom

Ask for clarity, patience, and the courage to do the right thing.

3

Wait and act

Read Scripture, seek counsel if needed, and take the next faithful step.

A simple example

God, I need wisdom about this decision. Show me what is true, help me trust you, and lead me in the next right step. Amen.

Silence

How to pray by sitting quietly before God.

Prayer is not always many words. Sometimes it is attention, stillness, and a willingness to listen.

1

Set a short timer

Take one or two minutes to sit still and stop trying to perform.

2

Turn attention to God

Breathe slowly, notice distractions, and gently return your attention to God.

3

End with one sentence

After the silence, speak one honest sentence of thanks, trust, or request.

A simple example

Lord, I am here. Help me listen, trust you, and rest in your care. Amen.

Prayer list

How to keep a simple prayer list or journal.

Writing requests down helps you remember who and what you are praying for, and it lets you notice answers later.

1

Write names and needs

Keep a small list of people, decisions, hurts, and things you are waiting on.

2

Pray from the list

Move through it slowly and keep the prayers short and specific.

3

Look back later

Review the list to notice what changed, what remained, and where God provided help.

A simple example

Friend: peace. Family: patience. Decision: wisdom. Body: healing. Thank God for what he has already done.

Work and study

How to pray before work, school, or study.

Prayer can steady your mind before a task. Ask for focus, honesty, patience, and the right use of your time.

1

Ask for focus

Pray for attention, clear thinking, and discipline before you begin.

2

Ask for integrity

Ask God to help you do honest work, speak truth, and treat people well.

3

Pause during the day

Use breaks, transitions, or deadlines as moments to pray again instead of pushing alone.

A simple example

God, help me focus, work honestly, and use this time well. Give me patience with people and wisdom in what I do. Amen.

Family and home

How to pray for your family, home, and daily relationships.

Home prayer is ordinary prayer for ordinary life: people you live with, care for, and see every day.

1

Name each person

Pray for family members, roommates, neighbors, or people you live with.

2

Ask for specific help

Pray for patience, safety, kindness, work, rest, and peace in the home.

3

Pray often, briefly

Use meals, mornings, and bedtimes as regular moments to remember them before God.

A simple example

God, please bless my home. Give patience, peace, and kindness to the people I live with. Amen.

Travel and safety

How to pray before a trip, commute, or errand.

Travel prayer can be brief and practical. Ask God for safety, alertness, and peace for the road or route ahead.

1

Name the route

Pray for the specific trip, drive, flight, train ride, or walk you are about to take.

2

Ask for safety

Pray for alertness, protection, and calm judgment for everyone involved.

3

Travel with trust

Use the trip as a reminder to depend on God instead of anxiety or control.

A simple example

God, please keep us safe on this trip. Give us alertness, peace, and a good arrival. Amen.

Witness

How to pray for people who do not yet know Jesus.

Prayer for witness is not pressure. It is asking God for open doors, courage, wisdom, and love so your life and words point to Christ well.

1

Name the people

Pray for friends, neighbors, coworkers, and family members by name, especially the ones you hope to speak with about faith.

2

Ask for open doors

Pray for good timing, clarity, courage, and a soft heart when opportunities come up.

3

Pray for love

Ask God to make you patient, truthful, and kind so your witness is shaped by Christ, not pressure or fear.

A simple example

Lord, help me love the people around me well. Open a door for honest conversation, give me courage to speak, and make my life point to Jesus. Amen.

Grief and suffering

How to pray when life hurts.

When you are grieving, afraid, or worn down, prayer can be a lament: honest about pain, honest about God, and still reaching for hope.

1

Say what hurts

Name the loss, fear, disappointment, or confusion without trying to make it sound nicer than it is.

2

Ask for help

Pray for comfort, endurance, mercy, and the kind of presence that only God can give.

3

Hold onto hope

Even a small sentence of trust can keep prayer alive when you do not have many words left.

A simple example

Lord, this hurts, and I do not know what to do. Be near to me, carry me through this, and keep my hope in you. Amen.

Death and dying

How to pray when someone is near death or has died.

Prayer in the face of death can be very simple: ask for mercy, ask for peace, and entrust the person to God’s care.

1

Ask for mercy

Pray for the person, for the family, and for God’s presence in the moment.

2

Ask for peace

Pray that fear would lessen and that comfort would be near.

3

Entrust them to God

Release what you cannot control and ask God to carry the person beyond what you can see.

A simple example

Lord, have mercy. Give peace to this person and their family, and hold them in your care. Amen.

Meals and moments

How to pray during meals, walks, and interruptions.

Prayer does not need a special room. Ordinary moments can become prayer when you pause and turn toward God.

1

Before meals

Thank God for the food and the people around you, then ask for blessing or peace.

2

During walks

Use a few steps or a few minutes to pray one sentence at a time.

3

In interruptions

When something surprises you, pause and say, help me, thank you, or have mercy.

A simple example

God, thank you for this meal. Bless the people here, and help me carry your peace into the next part of the day. Amen.

Addendum: Common Worship

How to use the Book of Common Worship as a support, not the center.

Use written prayer books after you know the Bible-based basics. They can help you borrow words for worship, confession, thanksgiving, and intercession when you need structure.

1

Start with Scripture

Let the Bible and the Lord’s Prayer set the shape of your prayer life first.

2

Borrow the words

Read the prayer book slowly and let it give you language when your own words run out.

3

Return to your own voice

Add one honest sentence of your own so the prayer stays personal and alive.

A simple example

Read one prayer from Common Worship, pause, add one sentence from your day, and end with Amen.
